Medical equipment financing, by the numbers

Market size, growth, lease mix, equipment costs, and the manufacturers whose finance arms fund the machines — from published sources.

Three different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures the financing pool: the size of the medical equipment financing market, counted by research firms with different scopes. The second kind measures the propensity to finance: how often American healthcare actually borrows to buy equipment, and in what form. The third kind measures the price of the machines themselves: what a scanner, a CT, or an X-ray room costs, new and refurbished. The first kind tells a reader how much financing is at stake. The second tells a reader how the borrowing happens. The third tells a reader why the borrowing is necessary at all.

What the latest numbers show is a market growing on every axis at once: the financing pool is projected to roughly double by the early 2030s, medical equipment is the most financed equipment type in the United States, and the machines themselves keep getting more expensive — while refurbished and pay-per-use models grow faster than the market as a whole.

How to read these numbers

Included: published medical equipment financing market sizes and forecasts, with the publisher's scope stated wherever estimates differ; the Equipment Leasing & Finance Association's U.S. financing propensity and mix figures; published 2026 equipment price bands for MRI and CT, new and refurbished; and the reported revenues of GE HealthCare, Siemens Healthineers, and Stryker, with the specialty lenders' published funding figures. Excluded: any single market-size headline presented without its scope or publisher; undated practitioner claims presented as dated figures; and any blending of the financing market with the underlying equipment market, which describe different things.

Published market-size estimates differ by scope — some count loans and leases only, others include lines of credit, pay-per-use, and refurbished equipment — so the range is shown wherever publishers disagree, and no single figure is presented as the only measure. Forecasts are labeled as forecasts, not actuals. Equipment prices are planning ranges from dealer cost guides, not quotes, and exclude siting unless stated. Lender funding totals are the lenders' own published figures as reported by the comparison sources cited.
