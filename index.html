The thesis

A name that states the purchase and the payment.

Medical equipment is the most financed equipment in America. In the Equipment Leasing & Finance Association's 2026 survey, 82% of medical equipment acquisitions were financed — the highest rate of any equipment type — and 80% of healthcare organizations used financing at all. A hospital does not buy a scanner the way a household buys a television. It signs a lease, a loan, or a managed-service contract, and the machine arrives on somebody else's balance sheet.

That is the business this name describes. "Medical device" is the word regulators use, and "medical equipment financing" is the phrase buyers actually type — the two meet in this domain. It is exact enough for a CFO to trust and plain enough for a practice owner to find.

The buyer pool is unusually deep for a three-word name. The manufacturers themselves run finance arms — Siemens Financial Services, GE HealthCare Financial Services, Philips Capital, Stryker Flex Financial — because financing is how they close equipment sales. The big banks run healthcare lending divisions. And a tier of specialty lenders — BHG Financial, National Business Capital, Credibly — exists precisely because the banks turn away the smaller practices that still need machines.

Specific, and broad enough to travel. The name covers imaging and surgical equipment, new and refurbished systems, leases and loans, hospitals and single-physician clinics. It is a finance name first and a medical name second — and in that order lies its value.
